Bathroom Water Damage in Rapid City

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. Our Rapid City team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

What It Looks Like

  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Damage Downstairs

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.
