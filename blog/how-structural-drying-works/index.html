How Structural Drying Works in Rapid City, SD

Home / Blog / Understanding Professional Structural Drying in Rapid City, SD

Few Rapid City, SD homeowners know much about structural drying, even though the rest of a restoration job depends on it. Pumping out the standing water still leaves a great deal of moisture inside the insulation, concrete, subfloor, wood framing, and drywall. Left in place, that moisture ends in odors, mold, and materials that break down. Below is a plain look at the science professionals rely on, and at why their results are much better than what DIY drying achieves.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

Professional structural drying is based on psychrometry, the study of the physical and thermodynamic properties of gas-vapor mixtures. For a drying job, that comes down to knowing how relative humidity, airflow, and temperature combine to move moisture out of a wet structure. IICRC-certified technicians use psychrometric calculations to determine the equipment setup each drying situation needs, including the type and number of dehumidifiers, the positioning of air movers, and the target temperature and humidity levels.

Drying Equipment Built for the Job

Professional drying gear is in a different class from anything sold for home use. A residential dehumidifier pulls 2 to 3 pints of water from the air each hour, while an LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) unit can pull 15 to 30 or more. Evaporation is sped up by air movers that deliver a directed, high-velocity stream of 2,500 CFM or more across wet surfaces. Desiccant dehumidifiers are used for specialized situations, including cold-weather drying and extreme moisture loads. This equipment represents tens of thousands of dollars in investment and requires specific training to deploy effectively.

Each Stage of Professional Drying

A professional drying job is done in a set order. It begins with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which are used to map every area the water reached. Technicians use the map to place equipment and to record starting moisture levels as a baseline. The crew then sets air movers and dehumidifiers where the psychrometric math says they will evaporate and capture the most moisture. Temperature may be raised to increase evaporation rates. Daily visits follow, with readings at each monitoring point, equipment adjustments where needed, and a record of how drying is going. Drying continues until all affected materials reach acceptable moisture levels compared to dry reference standards.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days with proper commercial equipment. The timeline depends on several factors: the volume of moisture present, the types of materials affected (concrete dries much slower than drywall), ambient conditions, building construction, and the number and type of equipment deployed. Wood framing typically dries in 3 to 4 days, standard drywall in 2 to 3 days, and concrete in 5 to 7 days or more. After the first moisture readings, the technician gives you an estimated schedule and updates it as the job moves along.

The Reason We Monitor Every Day

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Only calibrated readings can tell you if a material is drying at all, if it is on pace, and if it has reached a safe moisture level. Deep in a material, moisture can remain at a dangerous level even though the surface feels dry when touched. Verifying it takes a moisture meter that has been calibrated. There is a paperwork benefit too: documented drying proves to an insurance company that the job met industry protocol, and that backs up your claim and your home's value.
